Most 3D printed products, including a lot of what shows up in golf accessories, are printed in PLA. It is the easiest filament to print with and it costs less, which is exactly why it is everywhere. It is also the wrong material for anything that lives outside. PLA softens in heat, turns brittle over time, and does not hold up well through repeated sun and moisture. For something that sits on a desk or hangs on a keychain, none of that matters. For a golf ball dispenser that lives in a backyard, a simulator bay, or the trunk of a car, it matters a great deal.

That is why our golf ball dispenser is printed in PETG instead.

Choosing a material by where it lives

Our approach is simple: the material depends on where the piece is going to sit. Indoor pieces like our fidget clickers are printed in PLA, the right material for desks and keychains. Golf ball dispensers live outside, so they are printed in PETG, a plastic that takes more tuning to print well but is built for direct sun, rain, and heat. Parts that need to flex, like our Strike Form trainer, get printed in TPU instead. Same workshop, three different materials, each chosen for the job the part actually has to do. We break down the full comparison on our PETG page, but the material choice comes down to a few specific properties.

What PETG actually resists

PETG keeps its strength in direct sun, where PLA turns brittle over time. It resists water, where PLA absorbs moisture. It holds its shape in summer heat, where PLA can warp and soften. Left outside across seasons, PETG is built for it. PLA is not, because PLA was never meant to be an outdoor material in the first place.

Tested, not just claimed

We do not print in PETG because it sounds better on a product page. We tested it. Parts were left in a hot car at 114 degrees Fahrenheit and kept their shape rather than warping. Two full dispenser sets have lived outdoors under Utah desert sun and daily sprinklers since testing began, and they are still dispensing balls the way they did on day one.

That kind of testing is standard practice in our workshop, not a one time exercise for this product. Every product here starts as a CAD model, gets test printed, and is adjusted until it works the way it should. Nothing ships until it has been test printed and checked, and if a part does not perform the way it is supposed to, we adjust the model and print it again before it ever reaches a customer. The 114 degree car test and the ongoing outdoor sets are simply that same process applied specifically to heat, sun, and water, because that is what a golf ball dispenser has to survive in real use.

Why this matters if you are buying one

A golf ball dispenser only earns its place if it survives where it lives. Choosing PETG over PLA is not a marketing detail, it is the difference between a tool you set up once and use for years and one that softens and cracks after a summer outside. A dispenser that warps in the heat will not hold its hopper shape, and a hopper that shifts stops feeding balls the way it should, which turns a practice tool into something you have to babysit.
